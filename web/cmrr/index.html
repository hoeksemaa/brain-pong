CMRR collapse — play with the leak

The enemy (60 Hz mains) is common-mode: the same voltage Vcm on both canthi. A differential amp is supposed to subtract it away — but only if the two electrode paths are identical. Any impedance mismatch ΔZ converts that common-mode into a fake differential signal before it reaches the chip. Drag the sliders and watch it bury a saccade.

Reading the panel. The equation is the small-mismatch approximation; the plot and readouts use the exact voltage-divider difference V+−V− = Vcm·Zin·(ZR−ZL)/((Zin+ZL)(Zin+ZR)), floored by a fixed chip CMRR of 110 dB (you can't reject better than the amplifier itself). Notch filters live downstream of this — they can't undo a conversion that already happened at the electrodes.
